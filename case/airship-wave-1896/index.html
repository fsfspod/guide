The Great Airship Wave

Catalog FG-001When November 1896 – May 1897Where California, then across the Midwest and Texas

Fifty years before 'flying saucers,' Americans saw mysterious airships.

Starting in Sacramento in November 1896, people reported a lighted, cigar-shaped airship moving slowly across the night sky. By spring 1897 the reports had spread east through Nebraska, Kansas, Iowa, Illinois and Texas. Newspapers printed eyewitness accounts next to tall tales of secret inventors, crash landings and even a dead 'Martian' pilot buried in Aurora, Texas. Nobody had built a working powered airship in America yet.

The ledger

What the record shows, what people say, and the best explanation available so far.

What's documented

  • Hundreds of newspaper reports from 1896 and 1897 survive.
  • Several newspapers later admitted to printing hoaxes, including fake letters and staged 'airship' lights.

What's claimed

  • A secret inventor had built a flying machine.
  • Visitors from Mars.

Best explanation so far

Historians point to a mix of hoaxes, newspaper competition, kites and balloons carrying lanterns, and bright planets such as Venus. The wave is a useful preview of 1947: people describe what the technology of their era makes them expect.
