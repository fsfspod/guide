Roswell

Catalog FG-007When July 1947Where Near Roswell, New Mexico

A 'flying disc' press release, a weather balloon retraction and a legend that took 30 years to form.

Rancher Mac Brazel found a field of debris of foil, rubber, tape and sticks and brought it to the sheriff. On July 8, 1947, the Roswell Army Air Field announced it had recovered a 'flying disc.' The next day General Roger Ramey's office said it was a weather balloon and showed the press the debris. The story faded until 1978, when retired officer Jesse Marcel told researcher Stanton Friedman the debris was unearthly. Books and witnesses followed, adding tales of bodies, a second crash site and threats.

Weigh the evidence

The ledger

What the record shows, what people say, and the best explanation available so far.

What's documented

  • The press release and its retraction made national news.
  • In 1994 the Air Force identified the debris as a Project Mogul balloon train, a then-secret program to detect Soviet nuclear tests.
  • In 1997 the Air Force tied the 'alien body' stories to 1950s crash-test dummy drops and an unrelated accident.
  • A 1995 GAO report found some Roswell base records from that era had been destroyed.

What's claimed

  • A craft crashed and alien bodies were recovered and hidden.
  • The weather balloon story was a cover.

Best explanation so far

The debris matches Project Mogul materials. The body stories surfaced decades later and mix in events from other years. AARO's 2024 historical review found no evidence for the crash-retrieval version.
